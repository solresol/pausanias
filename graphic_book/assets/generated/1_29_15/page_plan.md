# Graves by the Academy · Pausanias 1.29.15

Pausanias groups the graves of Conon and Timotheus, Zeno, Chrysippus, the painter Nicias, Harmodius and Aristogeiton, and the orators Ephialtes and Lycurgus. The Academy Road ties these people together. One roadside reconstruction gives the list a setting, with a particular archaeological anchor in Lycurgus’s excavated family plot.

Arrington describes a poros wall facing the road and an attached funerary base set at an angle to address travellers approaching from the Academy. His note 115 records a competing location nearby for Lycurgus’s public burial. The image reconstructs the family enclosure and adjacent road. The caption leaves the precise grave Pausanias encountered open, and the other markers are anonymous. [Nathan T. Arrington, “Topographic Semantics,” p. 520 and n. 115](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/hesperia/41012853.pdf)

## Three designs considered

1. **Selected.** An uninterrupted road-level painting with two diagonally offset reading fields inset over quiet parts of the scene. The upper-left field introduces Athens and the father-and-son burials. The lower-right field continues the list and captions the reconstruction. Between the two, the road and the angled funerary base stay visible. The approach from the Academy gives the reader the viewpoint the turn of the stone addresses.
2. A full-width frieze of differentiated burial markers over one continuous English block. The long profile would give the cemetery presence, but regular spacing and differentiated markers could imply a documented sequence for the people listed. The profile also makes the base’s angle to approaching travellers less apparent.
3. A tall central study of the road-facing corner, with separate prose wings. The masonry would read clearly at close range, but the road would lose depth, and the arrangement would resemble 1.29.11’s memorial portrait between two reading wings.

The diagonal reading fields lie over one continuous painting. In the candidate-plus-five window, this is the only page with those masses. Counting every full-bleed painting with inset translation together, the window contains two, 1.29.10 and this page. Both fields are shallow relative to their width. The five predecessors have no full-height left translation sidebar, and this page uses none. The two immediate predecessors put the reading in one tall uninterrupted column beside one or two discrete views.
