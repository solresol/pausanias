Create a single richly observed naturalistic historical oil painting for a
fine-press Pausanias book, painted edge to edge at a LANDSCAPE 1.28:1 ratio
(3200 wide by 2500 high composition). Depict ancient Perinthus, c. 340 BCE,
on the north coast of the Propontis, during the period of Philip II’s siege.
Use a distant, low oblique viewpoint just above the sheltered harbour water,
looking toward a Greek port fortified on a distinctive high rocky peninsula.
Show a narrow, naturally dry neck connecting the peninsula with low Thracian
mainland at the left. A small low masonry defence marks the land approach.
The walled settlement rises toward the right out of the sea. Many fine,
differently angled late Classical small stone and plaster houses and muted
terra-cotta tile roofs climb the rocky slope in tightly spaced tiers, like
seats of a theatre. The tiering and the slender land connection must be
easy to distinguish. Use simple squared Greek rubble and ashlar defences,
modest rooflines, low dry scrub and exposed weathered coast. A small dark
wooden merchant hull with one plain furled square sail may quietly mark the
middle distance in the left harbour, subordinate to the shape of the coast.
The scene is about the city’s natural protection and maritime access.

Compose the essential buildings, isthmus and any vessel across the middle
between 23% and 62% of image height, within the central 84% of width to allow
for modest horizontal cropping. Keep the top 16% of the canvas a pale
quiet morning sky with soft slate-pearl haze across both left and right.
Below about 65% of the height, keep the centre and sides open harbour water
with subdued subtle ripples and no significant objects, ships or land.
The centre of that water will be covered by typeset prose added later.
Leave the actual water continuous across the bottom edge, with subtle
painterly short waves and depth. Keep title sky and water natural.

Use clear early morning side light coming gently across the clustered
buildings, pale shell-grey limestone underlit at the upper edges, muted
terracotta highlights, grey-green scrub, sea-glass teal and iron-blue water
with fine reflections, arresting near-to-far atmospheric depth. Avoid a
golden sunset, heavy ochre, uniform sepia and flat illustration. Give precise
varied stone faces, roof tiles, warm plaster, rock strata and sensitive
water treatment the material finish of an archival oil-painted book plate.
Maintain restraint and natural scale. No aerial map, collage, insets,
crests, labels, type, lettering, parchment, borders or graphic elements.
No battle action, combatants in close-up, portrait, flames, smoke spectacle,
blood, exposed bodies, monumental fantasy buildings, Roman arches, medieval
castles, modern harbour engineering, or invented landmark. Render only the
one coherent painting with no text at all.
