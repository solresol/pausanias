## Composition choices

1. **Selected.** One edge-to-edge maritime cityscape from low over the
   Propontis, with a wide, continuous English block inset over open water.
   The shoreline runs across the upper middle and the sea remains visible on
   both sides and below the prose card. The shelf of roofs and the landward
   neck explain Perinthus's position at a glance. A title and ID sit in the
   high open sky, and a short caption shares the parchment. The single scenic
   panel has no locator, second scene, or callout.
2. A vertical architectural portrait of the headland, flanked by two numbered
   prose wings. A tall central view would give the uphill roofs prominence but
   narrow the view enough to obscure the relationship of the isthmus, city and
   harbour. Dividing the prose would add a reading step to the short passage.
3. Two vertically stacked panels, a broad view of the siege setting and a
   close funerary stone on the Academy road, with a single prose block between
   them. This could give visual weight to the burial setting, but the passage
   supplies no details of the memorial and an imagined stone would compete
   with the strongest specific feature of the story. Interleaving bands also
   brings the large masses closer to 1.29.8.

The immersive scene with an embedded prose block occurs twice in the
candidate-plus-five window, counting 1.29.7 conservatively. Its central inset
has sea on three sides and the sky carries the heading. The 1.29.7 rectangle
meets the frame and creates an L-shaped picture. At thumbnail size 1.29.8 has
three solid horizontal bands and 1.29.9 has four alternating corner masses.
The proposed page has a single continuous painting and no tall left prose.

## Subjects and historical orientation

Pausanias recalls the Athenian commander Apollodorus and his foreign auxiliaries
sent by Arsites to protect Perinthus against Philip. The burial notice joins
him with Euboulos and men implicated in opposition to Lachares or the scheme
to take the Macedonian-held Piraeus. The complete English carries these
distinct people, places and events in its original order. A low maritime
view focuses on the defended city and avoids assigning a likeness or showing
the Athenian burials and conspiracies as part of the siege.

Diodorus describes the sieges, the reinforcements and supplies, the narrow
isthmus, and the view of the homes rising in tiers. The excavation survey
locates the acropolis on the promontory at present-day Marmara Ereğlisi, on the
north shore of the Propontis. The position, buildings and harbour traffic are
an imagined view of the fourth-century setting. The caption identifies the
historical basis. [Diodorus 16.74–76](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diodorus_Siculus/16D*.html#74) [Perinthos Topographic Plan](https://hdl.handle.net/20.500.14124/5714) [Perinthos Excavations](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352409X26005298)

Against the previous page's troops and cavalry on open hills, the painting
uses one distant, near-waterline oblique view of dense coastal architecture.
Cool sea-glass green, iron blue and shell-grey replace its dry olive and
mauve. Clear early-morning side light lights the stepped masonry and keeps the
sea shaded, a separate lighting choice from the cloud-softened battle views.

## Final visual comparison

The final PNG and its native-size details show tiered roofs climbing from the
landward saddle to the upper acropolis, with a small ship for harbour scale.
Water reflections, roof tiles, cliffs and stonework retain close detail.
The low angle gives the peninsula a pronounced profile, and the shaded
iron-blue foreground contrasts with the gently lit masonry. The upper
colonnaded building, walls and vessel are imagined details of the period.
The caption clarifies that the burial ground in the text is in Athens.

The second contact sheet compares the finished page with 1.29.4–1.29.9 in
numeric order. It confirms open water on both sides and below the prose,
different large shapes from the L-shaped cemetery page and the two immediate
predecessors, and the absence of a tall left prose sidebar. The composition
and text fit passed alongside the full-size art, suitability and orientation
checks. The reader and final PDF page preserve the visible arrangement.
