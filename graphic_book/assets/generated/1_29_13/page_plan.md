# 1.29.13 — The names along the road

Pausanias moves from one public memorial stele to the burials nearby. The
campaigns named in the reading stretch from mainland Greece and Thrace to
the Hellespont, Sicily and Cyprus. The picture keeps the reader on the
Academy road outside Athens, where the passage brings those places together.
The road and stone are imagined. The arrangement follows the broad
archaeological evidence for memorials beside the road and does not assign
a surviving marker, precise grave or inscription to the people in the text.

## Three compositions considered

1. **Selected.** Two unequal art fields flank the central reading. A large,
   raised view explains how the roadside monuments relate to one another.
   A smaller, lower-set study brings the reader close to the material of the
   stele. The prose proceeds down the same central measure from the notice on
   the stele to the burials. The two pictures show different scales of the
   same setting, with no map, inset overlay or leader lines.
2. A broad Mediterranean relief map with markers keyed to the campaigns and
   the English in a wide lower band. The range of places makes the map
   attractive, but plotting each episode would imply geographic precision the
   passage does not give, and the layout echoes 1.29.12’s two broad bands.
3. Three horizontal pairs of campaign scenes and corresponding text. This
   would make the long list easy to segment, but picturing a few campaigns
   would privilege them over the rest and bring too many invented events into
   what is primarily a visit to memorials.

The chosen composition occurs once in the candidate-plus-five window.
It has a central text mass between unlike art fields, while 1.29.11 has a
central picture between two reading wings and 1.29.12 has broad horizontal
bands. No tall left translation sidebar is used.

## Pre-art geometry and direction

The page is 3936 × 2350. The road panel is 1430 × 1790 (0.799:1), with a
high oblique view down the road, two or three distinct clusters of low
communal enclosures and a prominent slab. Dry wheat grass, wine-red earth,
pale mineral stone and deep cypress-green shadows give clear morning light
and a warmer color structure than the diffuse, silver-green river in 1.29.12.
The emphasis moves from figures across a river to ground, stone, and the
spatial relationship of the burial precincts.

The 828 × 1220 (0.679:1) study approaches the edge and back of a single slab
at close range. The corner, tooling and scattered mineral flecks carry the
view. Both paintings have clear margins for the final crop. Neither contains
text or a reserved lettering area. The page heading names Athens and the
Academy road and each panel has its own restrained local caption.

The translation is divided only after the first complete sentence, at the
break before “Buried here, too,”. The pre-art 59 px English passed all seven
field checks with 24 px of glyph padding. During visual review, I increased
the body to 61 px to give the last line more than one word. The two blocks
still wrap to 8 and 12 lines. Rejoining them reproduces all 726 characters
and 115 words of the exported reading, whose SHA-256 is
`e0a04a62f3900fa56a7920777cb825c3103f1914b14d256b67588afc7deee01b`.

## Final comparison

The native view shows the public enclosures on both sides of the diagonal
road. The setting reads in depth, from the foreground plinth and angled wall
to the distant markers and hills. The close panel shows the reverse and
worked bevel of a stele with a mottled mineral surface. The two pictures
share rust-colored ground, pale stone and defined shadows in warm daylight.
The exact enclosures, vegetation and stone profiles are interpretive.

Compared at thumbnail size with 1.29.7–1.29.12, the left picture, central
reading and lower-set right detail have a distinct outline. The layout
appears once in the six-page window and there is no left translation
sidebar. The raised view of stone precincts and the close material study
change both scale and center of attention from 1.29.12's eye-level figures.
The clear, warm light and cypress shadows also separate it from that cool,
diffuse riverscape. The final PNG passed the raster, historical orientation,
content, caption and measured-text gates.
