# Page plan: passage 1.21.6

## Passage

**ID:** 1.21.6

Pausanias explains how nomadic Sarmatians manufacture breastplates from cleaned,
split horse hoof: the pieces overlap like serpent scales or the scales of a green
pine cone, are pierced and sewn with horse or ox sinew, and form armour he judges
both beautiful and resistant to blows at close range and from afar.

## Key places, events, and objects

- The north-Pontic Sarmatian steppe, where horses support mobility, warfare,
  ritual, food, and material craft.
- Cleaned and split hoof pieces prepared as individual armour scales.
- Piercing and sinew-lacing the scales into overlapping rows.
- The green pine cone as Pausanias' visual analogy for the construction.
- The completed hoof-scale breastplate and its claimed protective strength.

## Main visual

A richly detailed Sarmatian leather-and-timber workshop on the open steppe. A
fully clothed craftsperson is caught mid-process lacing pale, translucent-brown
hoof scales into a breastplate laid over a shaped support. The foreground shows
the production sequence—clean hoof pieces, splitting, drilled holes, sinew, and
overlapping rows—without gore or carcass imagery. A finished cuirass and the
horse herd beyond the tent connect craft, material, and nomadic life.

## Scenic insets

1. **Pine-cone analogy:** a sophisticated natural-history still life pairing a
   fresh green pine cone with several pierced hoof scales and a small sample of
   finished overlapping armour, allowing the repeated geometry to be compared.
2. **Nomadic horse economy:** a historically grounded Sarmatian camp and moving
   horse herd across grassland, with felt tents, tack, and fully clothed riders;
   the scene supplies geographic and social context rather than depicting
   sacrifice or butchery.

## Orientation strategy

A subordinate textured relief strip links Athens, the Black Sea, and the
north-Pontic steppe. Local overlays add exact labels. Main-panel callouts identify
the cleaned hoof scales, piercing, sinew stitching, overlapping construction,
and finished cuirass; inset leaders identify the pine-cone scales, matching
armour geometry, horse herd, and mobile camp.

## Evidence boundary and suitability

Pausanias supplies the nomadic setting, many horses, their military, ritual, and
food uses, the hoof preparation, pine-cone comparison, piercing, sinew sewing,
and assessment of beauty and protective strength. Workshop architecture,
clothing, tools, individual makers, herd arrangement, and the exact form of the
finished cuirass are reconstructions. All figures must be fully clothed. No
slaughter, blood, carcasses, gore, nudity, pseudo-text, or modern equipment.
