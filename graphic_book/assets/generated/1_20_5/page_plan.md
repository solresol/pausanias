## Subject and historical boundaries

Pausanias is the narrative source: Aristion's embassies, divided Athenian allegiance, Roman victory, flight to Athens, withdrawal to Piraeus, and earlier fighting near Sipylus. Illustrative broad terrain connects inland Athens and coastal Piraeus; figures suggest retreat without a located battle, specific formations, named portraits or violence. Do not invent a confrontation between notables and a Roman delegation. The distant Long Walls corridor may be suggested but no complete wall condition is asserted. Labels identify destinations, not precise troop routes. Sipylus is in western Asia Minor, outside this Attic scene.

Orientation support consulted 2026-09-15: ASCSA, Anna Theocharaki, The Ancient Circuit Wall of Athens, https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/hesperia/hesp.80.1.0071.pdf — the Long Walls connected city and port; this is a broad orientation painting, not a measured topographic reconstruction. Exact coastline, road geometry, buildings and troop positions remain illustrative.

Compared with 1.20.4, change camera distance from close architectural cutaway to expansive coast/plain; palette from ochre/brown to silver blue, muted sage and limestone grey; lighting from golden sun to cool diffuse overcast; subject balance from monumental architecture to landscape and tiny moving groups. These choices support spatial separation and the withdrawal after defeat. One rich raster scene, no decorative insets or schematic locator. Figures clothed, no wounds, bodies or gore.
