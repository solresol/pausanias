Use case: finished historical raster illustration for a Pausanias graphic-book page.
Asset: one wide upper panel, initially planned at 2.19:1 and finally cropped at 1.95:1 after page review. Leave all text out; local typesetting will supply it.
Scene: late fourth-century BCE Athens viewed from a high rocky vantage east of the Museum hill, looking southwest. The Museum hill is the near-middle-ground subject, with restrained low Hellenistic stone fortification and a few small fully clothed garrison figures integrated into the ridge. Across the valley, the Acropolis is recognisable on the right. Ancient urban fabric, winding terrain, cypress and Attic scrub, distant atmospheric land toward the Saronic coast. This is an interpretive reconstruction, not a claim to know exact lost buildings.
Style: exceptionally rich, sophisticated painterly historical landscape, tactile masonry and vegetation, carefully layered depth, credible Greek topography and architecture, coffee-table-book quality. Wide panoramic composition, visual subject clear at thumbnail scale.
Lighting/palette: cool blue-lilac dawn, pale silver light, deeper cypress and shadowed marble. No warm ochre sunset.
Constraints: no Roman Philopappos monument, no invented identifiable tomb, no staged killing or battle, no modern/medieval architecture, no temple on the Museum hill, no anachronistic battlements, no nudity, no writing, captions, labels, signage or watermark.
