# Page plan — Pausanias 1.20.3

## Passage

- **Passage ID:** 1.20.3
- **Key places:** the oldest sanctuary of Dionysus beside the Theatre of Dionysus on the south slope of the Acropolis, its enclosure, and Olympus in the painted myth.
- **Key events and objects:** two temples and two images of Dionysus (Dionysus Eleuthereus and Alcamenes' ivory-and-gold image); the painted return of Hephaestus after Dionysus makes him drunk; Hera bound to the golden throne; Pentheus and Lycurgus punished; Ariadne asleep as Theseus sails and Dionysus arrives.

## Visual design

- **Main visual:** a richly painted oblique reconstruction of the sanctuary beside the Theatre of Dionysus. The theatre's curving seating, the Acropolis cliff, and the sanctuary enclosure provide unmistakable orientation. Within the enclosure, two distinct compact temples stand apart: one housing the fully robed Dionysus Eleuthereus and another revealing Alcamenes' fully draped ivory-and-gold Dionysus. - **Inset 1:** the central painted myth: fully robed Dionysus leads an intoxicated but dignified, fully clothed Hephaestus back toward the Olympian assembly, while Hera remains visibly bound to an ornate golden throne in the background. The scene is non-comic and non-violent.
- **Inset 2:** Ariadne asleep in full drapery on the Naxian shore; Theseus' ship is already departing while fully robed Dionysus approaches with a restrained procession. The spatial triangle makes the simultaneous painted episode readable without eroticism.
