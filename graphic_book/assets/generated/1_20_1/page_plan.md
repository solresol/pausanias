# Page plan — Pausanias 1.20.1

## Passage

- **Passage ID:** 1.20.1
- **Key places:** the Prytaneion quarter, the Street of the Tripods, the eastern and southern approach around the Acropolis, and the small choragic monuments that gave the street its name.
- **Key events and objects:** bronze tripods bearing memorable reliefs; Praxiteles' highly prized satyr; Phryne asking the sculptor to name his finest work; and her servant falsely announcing a workshop fire to reveal which sculpture Praxiteles most feared losing.

## Visual design

- **Main visual:** a richly painted oblique street-level reconstruction of the Street of the Tripods in second-century CE Athens. The view proceeds from the Prytaneion quarter past a sequence of compact temple-like choragic monuments crowned by bronze tripods, with the Acropolis rising above for unmistakable geographic orientation. The monuments, tripods, relief panels, pedestrians, stone paving, workshops, and urban slope form a sophisticated historical cityscape rather than a diagram. No text is baked into the art.
- **Inset 1:** a close museum-like study of Praxiteles' fully draped or otherwise professionally suitable satyr sculpture in a small sanctuary niche, with the bronze tripod and relief-bearing monument visible behind it. The satyr is the unmistakable subject and contains no nudity.
- **Inset 2:** Phryne's servant rushing into Praxiteles' active sculpture workshop with the false fire alarm; fully clothed Phryne watches as Praxiteles turns toward his most valued works in alarm. Marble figures, tools, dust, warm daylight, and a faint non-dangerous haze make the anecdote visually legible without text or destructive spectacle.
- **Orientation strategy:** locally rendered leaders identify the Acropolis, Prytaneion direction, paved Street of the Tripods, choragic monuments, bronze tripods, and relief panels. A restrained orientation strip states the route from Prytaneion toward the theatre precinct beneath the Acropolis.

## Evidence and suitability

- Pausanias supplies the street's origin at the Prytaneion, its name, the small temples, bronze tripods, reliefs, Praxiteles' prized satyr, Phryne's request, their relationship, and the servant's false report of workshop fire. Exact street alignment, monument forms, viewpoint, architectural details, clothing, workshop contents, and momentary gestures are reconstructed.
- All figures are fully clothed and professionally suitable. The satyr study is fully draped and non-erotic. Generated art contains no exact text; passage text, captions, labels, and leaders are added locally with measured layout.
