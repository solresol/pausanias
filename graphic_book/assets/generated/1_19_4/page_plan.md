# Page plan: passage 1.19.4

- **Passage ID:** 1.19.4
- **Key places, events, and objects:** the tomb of Nisus behind the Lyceum in Athens; Nisus as king of Megara; the Cretan invasion; Nisus taking refuge in the fortified harbour of Nisaea; Minos directing the siege; Nisus' purple life-preserving lock; his daughter Scylla cutting it after falling in love with Minos.
- **Main visual:** a richly painted oblique historical reconstruction looking from the Lyceum grove toward Athens, with the tomb of Nisus prominent beside the sacred road and the Acropolis providing geographic orientation. The tomb is treated as a commemorative monument rather than an archaeologically certain reconstruction.
- **Scenic insets:** (1) the Cretan siege of the walled harbour of Nisaea, with ships, walls, and Megarian terrain; (2) a restrained nocturnal interior in which Scylla cuts the distinctive purple lock from the sleeping, fully clothed Nisus while Minos' distant siege fires remain visible.
- **Orientation strategy:** the main panel fixes the monument behind the Lyceum relative to the Acropolis and Athens; the siege inset identifies Nisaea as Megara's fortified harbour and visually connects land and sea attack; a small locally rendered route strip links Athens, Megara, and Nisaea without allowing a schematic map to dominate.
- **Evidence boundary:** Pausanias records the tomb and the myth. The monument's form, the precise placement within the Lyceum landscape, the siege arrangement, clothing, architecture, and the nocturnal betrayal scene are visual reconstructions.
