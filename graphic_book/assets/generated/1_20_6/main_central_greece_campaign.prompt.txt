Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: main raster art panel for a scholarly illustrated page
Primary request: Create a richly painted oblique campaign landscape of central Greece in 86 BCE, looking south from Phocis through the Cephisus valley and Boeotia toward distant Attica. The fortified hill town of Elateia sits in the upper left distance; Chaeronea and its rugged foothills occupy the middle; the broad Boeotian plain, Lake Copais wetlands, roads, olive groves, and mountain passes lead toward a faint Athens and Acropolis on the far southern horizon. Small, clearly separated bodies of fully clothed troops travel on different roads: Taxilus's Pontic army withdraws from Elateia toward Boeotia, while Sulla's late-Republican Roman force advances north from Attica. Several mounted messengers ride between the routes. Geography and strategic movement dominate, not combat.
Scene/backdrop: central Greek valleys and passes under layered Parnassus, Helicon, and Cithaeron-like mountain masses, with cultivated plains and atmospheric distance.
Subject: the converging marches between Elateia, Chaeronea, Boeotia, and Athens, plus the divided Roman command and messenger network.
Style/medium: sophisticated historical gouache and watercolor over fine ink, naturalistic archaeological reconstruction, tactile parchment-atlas palette, museum-publishing quality, strong topographic depth and atmosphere.
Composition/framing: wide landscape with a high oblique viewpoint; keep Elateia, Chaeronea, Boeotia, and distant Athens spatially distinct; leave useful clear zones around the upper and side edges for later locally rendered labels and leaders; no decorative frame.
Lighting/mood: crisp early-morning light with long shadows, purposeful movement, tense but observational.
Materials/textures: limestone ridges, dusty roads, olive and scrub vegetation, terracotta roofs, pale marsh water, worn cloth, bronze and iron equipment.
Constraints: no text, no labels, no symbols, no arrows, no border, no watermark; all people small, fully clothed, and appropriate to the early first century BCE; Romans wear late-Republican mail and helmets; Pontic troops use varied Hellenistic equipment; the landscape must read as rich scenic art rather than a diagram.
Avoid: gore, corpses, exposed bodies, modern settlements, medieval armour, imperial segmented armour, fantasy citadels, flat vector terrain, classroom diagram, icon figures, schematic map, sparse empty scene.
