# Page plan — Pausanias 1.20.6

- **Passage ID:** 1.20.6.
- **Key places, events, and objects:** the Athenian siege; Taxilus leaving the siege of Elateia in Phocis and marching toward Attica; Sulla dividing his army and advancing through Boeotia; the Roman victory near Chaeronea; news that the wall of Athens had fallen; the detention of resisting Athenians in the Cerameicus and selection by lot.
- **Main visual:** a richly painted oblique campaign landscape across central Greece in 86 BCE, looking south from Phocis through the Cephisus valley and Boeotia toward Attica. Elateia, Chaeronea, the Boeotian plain, mountain passes, and distant Athens are visually distinct. Small period armies move on separate roads while mounted messengers connect the divided Roman camps. Exact place labels, leaders, and route explanations are added locally.
- **Inset 1:** a non-graphic Chaeronea battle tableau in the foothills, with Sulla's late-Republican Roman line overcoming Taxilus's Pontic force. Terrain, formations, and command gestures carry the scene; there are no bodies, wounds, or gore.
- **Inset 2:** a restrained Cerameicus aftermath scene: fully clothed Athenian prisoners are guarded within the cemetery district while an officer draws marked lots from a ceramic vessel. The threatened execution remains off-page.
- **Orientation strategy:** the main panel establishes the north-to-south sequence from Elateia through Chaeronea and Boeotia toward Athens. Locally rendered route strokes distinguish Taxilus's march, Sulla's counter-march, and the messenger network; the two insets then resolve the battle and urban aftermath.

Exact routes, viewpoints, troop formations, clothing, gestures, architecture, and the appearance of the lots are reconstructions.
