# Page plan — passage 1.18.6

## Passage and evidence

- **Passage:** 1.18.6
- **Key place:** the sanctuary of Olympian Zeus (the Olympieion), southeast of the Acropolis in Athens.
- **Key objects and events:** Hadrian's dedication of the completed temple and colossal gold-and-ivory Zeus; four stone statues of Hadrian before the sanctuary; bronze figures called "colonies" before the columns; a roughly four-stade enclosure filled with civic dedications; the Athenians' own colossal Hadrian behind the temple.
- **Evidence boundary:** Pausanias supplies the dedication, materials, relative scale, statue groupings, and enclosure measurement. The precise appearance, placement, colours, ceremony, and crowd are reconstructed. The locator marks broad historical relationships rather than a surveyed second-century plan.

## Visual hierarchy

- **Main visual:** elevated oblique reconstruction of the completed Olympieion and its statue-filled precinct under Hadrian. The temple and enclosure dominate; the Acropolis remains visible in the middle distance for immediate orientation. Numerous distinct stone and bronze statues make the passage's exceptional density visible without turning the art into a diagram.
- **Inset 1 — locator:** textured relief of central Athens with the Acropolis, Olympieion, and Ilissos corridor. This is subordinate orientation, with exact markers and labels added locally.
- **Inset 2 — Zeus:** cinematic cella interior focused on the enormous seated chryselephantine Zeus, with small fully clothed visitors providing scale.
- **Inset 3 — imperial precinct:** ceremonial approach showing two pale Thasian-marble and two dark Egyptian-stone Hadrians, plus a line of varied bronze civic personifications before Corinthian columns. This clarifies the materials and the "colonies" without relying on the caption alone.

## Orientation and callouts

- Main-panel callouts identify the Olympieion, Corinthian colonnade, four-stade enclosure, city dedications, and the colossal Athenian Hadrian behind the temple.
- The locator establishes the Acropolis–Olympieion–Ilissos relationship.
- Inset leaders point directly to the ivory-and-gold surfaces of Zeus, the four stone Hadrians, and the bronze colony figures.
