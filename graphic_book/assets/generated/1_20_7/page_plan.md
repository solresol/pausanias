# Page plan — Pausanias 1.20.7

## Passage

`1.20.7`

The passage moves from Sulla's continuing anger against Athens to a secret appeal at Delphi, the Pythia's oracle about the wineskin, Sulla's later disease, Pausanias' rejection of simple retributive causation, the alternative explanation of Hikesios' wrath after Aristion was dragged from Athena's sanctuary, and Athens' later recovery under Hadrian.

## Key places, events, people, and objects

- Athens after the Roman sack, 86 BCE
- Delphi and the Pythia's oracle about the wineskin
- Sulla, Aristion, and the violated sanctuary of Athena
- Hikesios as the divine power whose wrath Pausanias considers
- Athens' renewed monumental life under Hadrian

## Main visual

A rich, historically grounded reconstruction outside an Athenian sanctuary of Athena: Aristion clings to a sacred boundary while late-Republican Roman soldiers under Sulla's authority pull him away. The Acropolis and damaged city establish Athens after the siege. The moment is tense but non-graphic, with no execution and no nudity. The architecture and objects do the historical work: sanctuary threshold, cult statue visible within, sacred olive, battered walls, smoke, late-Republican mail, bronze helmets, oval/scutum shields, pila, and standards. Exact labels and leaders are rendered locally.

## Scenic insets

1. **Delphi oracle:** the Pythia seated on a tripod in Apollo's sanctuary, laurel and omphalos visible, while a small group of weary Athenian envoys listens; an ordinary tied leather wineskin is placed as a visual oracle motif. No generated writing.
2. **Hadrianic recovery:** a sunlit second-century view of renewed Athens, with the Olympieion and Hadrianic building activity in the foreground and the Acropolis behind. The inset makes the passage's final temporal reversal immediately legible.
