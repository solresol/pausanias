# The works of Lycurgus · Pausanias 1.29.16

## Passage and orientation

Pausanias juxtaposes Lycurgus’s public revenue, dedications to Athena, military preparation and building works with the later removal of precious objects by Lachares. The two pictures show reconstructions of fourth-century BCE work: the Zea shipsheds at Piraeus and a selection of dedications for the Acropolis. The English itself supplies the theatre, Lyceum, removal and survival. The 6,500-talent comparison with Pericles belongs to Pausanias’s account. The decree gives separate revenue and advance figures. [Decree honouring Lycurgus, including the literary version at 852](https://atticinscriptions.com/inscription/Sylloge/326)

The decree’s literary version describes solid-gold Victories, gold and silver ceremonial vessels, gold ornaments for basket-bearers, and a fleet including repaired and newly built triremes. Depict a repair scene and reserve uncertain details of the lost objects for an explicitly labelled interpretive study. The older Parthenon frieze is a comparison for Attic processional vessel forms and drapery. Lovén’s excavation documents elongated, colonnaded shipsheds under tiled roofs. Pakkanen models a 6.5 m bay width and a roughly 59 m single slipway in one Phase 3 reconstruction. The painted view shows only a section of the bays and leaves open the full length and arrangement. ## Three compositions considered

1. **Selected.** A wide shipshed interior below a shallow title margin. Beneath the painting and caption, the full English flows left to right through two measured columns beside a compact, independently captioned study of the Acropolis dedications. The long slipway orients the naval work, and the tight object view lets the reader see the difference between valuable movable objects and enduring installations. No locator or callout lines are needed.
2. A large central gold Victory on a stone table with the English divided into two outer wings. The figure would give the loss immediate force, but spatial context for the dockyards would be reduced and the layout would echo 1.29.11.
3. Three ordered vignettes of sanctuary fittings, fleet preparation, and buildings, with short prose blocks below each. This would offer more literal coverage but fragment the continuous reading, reduce the space for the long shipshed, and suggest a precision the locations and objects do not support.

The large upper art and lower row of two text columns beside an object plate occur once in the candidate-and-five window. Even counting all full-width-image-with-separate-reading-band pages together, 1.29.12 is the only other instance. The two most recent pages use a full-height side division and diagonal inset cards respectively. The candidate has no tall left translation sidebar.

From 1.29.15, shift from a free-standing outdoor approach to a cross-bay interior view on human scale. Repeated stone supports and a wooden hull now carry the scene. Cool blue-grey harbour light and charcoal timber replace green and violet, and reflected overcast illumination takes the place of low early sun. The small warm-metal study gives the page a second material register without making the dominant art ochre.

## Final comparison

The full-page proof and native-size crops show clear recessed bays, parallel slipways, roof tiles, wet stone and the change of grain around the repair plank. The harbour opening keeps the working interior legible as part of Piraeus. The revised raster continues the bare ridge beyond the harbour where the first proof suggested a particular temple. The revised still life depicts a draped gold Victory, a gold ewer, a silver bowl and a small gold fillet. Its caption marks the objects as reconstructions. The theatre, Lyceum and later removal and survival remain in the complete reading.

The final thumbnail comparison covers 1.29.10–1.29.15.
