## Compositions considered

1. **Selected.** An asymmetric diagonal diptych with offset image widths and
   numbered English in the opposite corners. Upper left shows Argive
   reinforcement and the upper-right English recounts the alliance and aid.
   Lower-right art focuses on the Thessalian cavalry and the lower-left
   English carries nightfall and the following day's reversal. The different
   scales and diagonal exchange make the temporal and political break visible.
2. A single wide landscape over the full passage. This would suit the troop
   convergence but compress two days and two interventions into one view.
   Showing one phase alone would underplay the reversal. The horizontal massing would
   recur from 1.29.6.
3. A tall cavalry equipment study with two short prose wings. The portrait
   would foreground objects that Pausanias does not describe and give the
   Argive contribution little visual weight. It would also echo 1.29.3's
   central object and prose wings.

In the window of 1.29.4 through 1.29.9, the diptych occurs once. It has neither
1.29.8's central panorama between prose bands nor 1.29.7's continuous art
wrapped around a prose island. The lower-left prose field occupies one corner
of one row, while art occupies the upper left. The closest earlier diagonal
diptych is 1.29.2, outside the six-page window.

## Subjects and orientation

The two scenes are imaginative, separate moments near Tanagra in Boeotia.
The exact exported English carries the alliance, opposing sides, Argive
advantage, interruption by night, and the reversal Pausanias attributes to
Thessalian betrayal. The scenes show the arrival of allied infantry and the
mounted contingent on open ground. Thucydides 1.107.5–1.108.1 also places
the fight at Boeotian Tanagra, reports the arrival of Argive infantry and
specifies that the Thessalian force that changed sides was cavalry. The
images supply no surveyed positions, identified commanders, military symbols
or invented episode of direct combat. A local location line and one caption
orient both scenes. There are no extra insets, maps or leaders.
[Thucydides, History 1.107–108](https://adfonteslibrary.org/work-thucydides-history)

Compared with the bright, ground-level departure column in 1.29.8, the
reinforcement is seen from a low rise at wide distance across layered open
ground. Cool slate, olive and mauve replace the clear blue and limestone
palette. The second panel changes to a close lateral view of mounted figures
in cool, pale daylight, with violet-grey shadows and soft ivory highlights.
Distinct infantry and horse silhouettes carry the contrast between the
reinforcement and reversal without relying on inscriptions or battle marks.

## Final review

The initial infantry raster brought a column and olive bough into the
foreground, making that scene too close to 1.29.8's viewpoint. The revision
keeps both groups in the middle distance on a diagonal hillside and restores
the intended landscape scale. The published layout uses the revised infantry
image and the first cavalry image. Both accepted raster crops are within a
tenth of one percent of their source aspect ratio. A third rider sits partly
against the lower panel's left edge. The foreground pair and their tack read
clearly, and the two visible spearheads remain within the frame.

The full 3100 × 2500 PNG and a same-scale thumbnail comparison against
1.29.3–1.29.8 passed. The alternating art and prose corners remain distinct
from the latest two layouts. The elevated wide view, cloud-softened lighting,
cool landscape color, and close mounted portrait change scale, angle and
subject weight from 1.29.8. The open hills and near-to-far material detail
meet the art standard set by 1.1.4 and 1.1.5. Every label and the caption
remains clear of its borders, and the caption names the relevant groups
and marks terrain and positions as imagined. The scene review found
period-appropriate visual cues, fully clothed figures and legible hierarchy.
