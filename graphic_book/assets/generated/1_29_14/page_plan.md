# 1.29.14 — Five Attic triremes

Pausanias moves along the Academy road in Athens, identifying the tombs of
the men the Athenians say aided Rome, Tolmides and his men, and those who
won on land and sea with Cimon. The picture follows one detail from the
first notice, the five Attic triremes. The location line keeps the reader
on the Academy road and the caption identifies the squadron as the
Athenians' account. Pausanias names neither the naval engagement nor its
location here. The picture imagines the ships at sea without situating them
at an identified port or showing a particular battle. The Tolmides and
Cimon notices remain in the complete reading with no implied link to the
ships depicted.

The form of the foreground vessel draws on the Hellenic Navy's
[experimental reconstruction of an Athenian trireme](https://averof.hellenicnavy.gr/en/trireme-olympias/construction-of-the-trireme-by-the-hn/).
It is an analogy for the long slim wooden hull, low draft, banks of
oars, bow ram and stern curve, with the precise appearance and arrangement
of the five historical vessels left open.

## Three compositions

1. **Selected.** An uninterrupted near-square maritime picture fills the
   left three-fifths at full height and runs to the top, left and bottom
   edges. At right the entire English follows the title and location down
   one continuous ivory reading field, with a short caption beneath. The
   near trireme has room to convey its hull, oars and wake. Four more
   identify the small squadron and establish distance. The strong two-part
   silhouette holds at thumbnail size. The page needs one painting and no
   insets or leader labels.
2. **Central stone with text wings.** A close Academy-road tomb would
   foreground what Pausanias sees, while the English could sit in separate
   wings. It would revisit the 1.29.11 silhouette, carry over the emphasis
   on grave markers from 1.29.13, and require conjecture about the tomb's
   exact form.
3. **Land-and-sea sequence.** Three horizontal strips would place the
   reading with the Roman support, Tolmides, and Cimon in turn. This
   suggests a shared sequence of action where the passage gives separate
   burials and could give speculative battles more weight than the
   Academy-road account.

The full-height art beside a single right reading differs from the upper
reading band of 1.29.12 and the three-part art–text–art arrangement of
1.29.13. It occurs once in the candidate and five predecessors, with zero
tall left translation sidebars. The lower viewpoint, from a boat near the
wave tops, replaces the raised road view and close stone study. Marine
jade and early light replace 1.29.13's warm limestone and afternoon-like
shadows. Long hulls in moving water replace static monuments.
