# Page plan: Pausanias 1.17.5

- **Passage ID:** 1.17.5
- **Key places, events, and objects:** the sanctuary and sacred oak of Zeus at Dodona; Cichyrus; Lake Acherusia; the Acheron and dark Cocytus rivers; Pausanias' claim that Homer transferred these Thesprotian names into his poetic underworld; the capture of Aphidna by the sons of Tyndareus and the installation of Menestheus at Athens.
- **Main visual:** A historically and geographically grounded, elevated dawn-to-storm landscape reconstruction of the Acheron valley near Cichyrus. Lake Acherusia, a pale Acheron channel, and the darker Cocytus converge beneath wooded Epirus mountains, with a small fortified Cichyrus on a ridge. Subtle mist and cave-like shadow evoke the later underworld association without turning the real landscape into fantasy.
- **Insets:** (1) a rich reconstruction of the Dodona sanctuary focused on the immense sacred oak, bronze dedications, priests, and mountain setting; (2) a dramatic but non-gory Mycenaean-era assault at Aphidna, with the sons of Tyndareus' force entering a breached hill-town gate; (3) a subordinate oblique relief landscape of north-west Greece and Attica for locally drawn orientation markers and route/context lines.
- **Orientation strategy:** Exact local labels and leaders identify Cichyrus, Acherusia, Acheron, Cocytus, and their confluence on the main landscape. The subordinate relief inset places Dodona and Cichyrus in Epirus and Aphidna near Athens. The page explicitly distinguishes the observed Thesprotian waters from Homer's poetic adoption of their names.
- **Reference pages:** `graphic_book/images/1/1/4.png` and `graphic_book/images/1/1/5.png` are the approved anchors for parchment-atlas framing, visual richness, local labels, and coffee-table-book impact. `graphic_book/images/1/17/4.png` is used only for immediate layout continuity.
- **Evidence boundary:** The ancient shorelines, river courses, settlement appearance, Dodona sanctuary furnishings, and Aphidna assault are historically informed reconstructions, not surveyed plans. The main panel illustrates Pausanias' geographical claim and does not assert that Homer visited a specific modern archaeological site.
- **Content suitability:** All figures are fully clothed. The military inset shows urgency and capture without gore, exposed wounds, humiliation, or eroticized poses.
- **Text policy:** Generated art contains no lettering. Passage text, labels, captions, leaders, route/context marks, and the reconstruction note are measured and rendered locally.
