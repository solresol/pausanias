# Page plan — Pausanias 1.21.3

## Passage

`1.21.3`

Pausanias turns from the Theatre of Dionysus to the Acropolis' south face: a gilded Gorgon head with an aegis on the Southern Wall, a rocky cave above the theatre crowned by a tripod, and an image inside of Apollo and Artemis attacking Niobe's children. He then compares this represented grief with the natural formation on Mount Sipylus, which is merely rock at close range but resembles a bowed, weeping woman from farther away.

## Key places, events, people, and objects

- The Theatre of Dionysus beneath the Acropolis
- The Acropolis wall called the Southern Wall
- A gilded head of Medusa set within an aegis
- The rocky cave at the top of the theatre and the tripod above it
- The cave image of fully clothed Apollo and Artemis pursuing Niobe's children, shown non-graphically
- Mount Sipylus and the distance-dependent appearance of the Weeping Rock

## Main visual

A richly painted, inhabited oblique reconstruction of the Acropolis south slope viewed from above the Theatre of Dionysus. The theatre occupies the foreground and fixes the viewpoint; the Southern Wall rises above it with a visible gilded Gorgon-and-aegis ornament. Among the rocks below the wall is the choragic cave, its entrance shadowed and its tripod silhouetted against the masonry. Warm late-afternoon light makes gold, limestone, theatre seating, cliff vegetation, and human scale immediately legible.

## Scenic insets

1. **Inside the cave:** a historically styled painted-relief or mural composition showing fully clothed Apollo and Artemis pursuing the children of Niobe. The moment is tense but non-graphic: no wounds, blood, bodies, or exposed flesh. The inset depicts the artwork Pausanias reports rather than presenting the myth as an unqualified historical event.
2. **Mount Sipylus, near and far:** one sophisticated landscape diptych. The near view reads as weathered cliff and wet mineral streaks; the distant view lets the same formation cohere into the silhouette of a bowed, grieving woman. The two viewpoints must remain visibly the same mountain feature.

## Orientation strategy

The main reconstruction uses the Theatre, south-slope cave, tripod, Southern Wall, and Acropolis crest as a vertical geographic sequence. A compact route key reads `THEATRE · CAVE AND TRIPOD · SOUTHERN WALL · ACROPOLIS`. The Mount Sipylus inset carries a locally rendered `NEAR / FAR` comparison and a small `LYDIA · MOUNT SIPYLUS` locator label, keeping Asia Minor orientation subordinate to the Athens main panel.

## Evidence boundary

Pausanias supplies the relative positions of theatre, Southern Wall, Gorgon-and-aegis, cave and tripod; the cave image's subject; and his near/far observation at Mount Sipylus. Exact architecture, ornament scale, cave decoration technique, costumes, spectators, lighting, vegetation, and selected viewpoints are reconstructions. The Mount Sipylus comparison is an interpretive visualization of Pausanias' reported perception, not a surveyed identification.
