# Page plan: Pausanias 1.18.1

- **Passage ID:** 1.18.1
- **Key places, events, people, and objects:** the ancient Athenian sanctuary of the Dioscuri (Anakeion); standing images of Castor and Polydeuces; their sons seated on horses; Polygnotus' painting of the marriage of the daughters of Leucippus and the Dioscuri's role; Micon's painting of Jason's expedition to Colchis, especially Acastus and his horses.
- **Main visual:** A richly reconstructed fifth-century-BCE sanctuary interior on the north slope beneath the Athenian Acropolis. Visitors enter a long painted hall where the standing cult figures of the Dioscuri and the mounted sons form the ritual centre, while two monumental wall paintings remain spatially legible: Polygnotus' marriage scene on one wall and Micon's Argonaut departure on the other. The composition uses architectural depth, lamplight, painted plaster, offerings, and the rocky Acropolis slope visible through the doorway to make this a place rather than an illustration chart.
- **Insets:** (1) a close scenic reconstruction of Polygnotus' marriage painting, with the fully clothed daughters of Leucippus, the Dioscuri, attendants, and wedding ritual; (2) a close scenic reconstruction of Micon's Argonaut painting, with Jason and the Argo at the shore but Acastus and his matched horses given deliberate foreground emphasis; (3) a subordinate oblique relief view of classical Athens and the Acropolis north slope for locally rendered labels marking the conjectural sanctuary zone, Acropolis, Agora, and route from the preceding Theseion area.
- **Orientation strategy:** The main panel identifies the sanctuary interior, cult images, mounted sons, and both mural walls with semantic leaders. The relief inset locates the sanctuary only as a conjectural north-slope zone and distinguishes it from the Agora and summit. Captions explicitly separate Pausanias' report from the reconstructed architecture and lost paintings.
