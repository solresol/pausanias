# Page plan — Pausanias 1.21.1

## Passage

`1.21.1`

The passage begins with the statues of tragic and comic poets in the Athenian theatre, distinguishes the famous Menander, Euripides, and Sophocles from more obscure poets, and then recounts how a Lacedaemonian commander dreamed that Dionysus ordered funeral honours for the newly dead “Siren,” understood as Sophocles and the charm of his poetry.

## Key places, events, people, and objects

- Theatre of Dionysus on the south slope of the Athenian Acropolis
- Statues of comic and tragic poets, especially Menander, Euripides, and Sophocles
- A Lacedaemonian invasion of Attica after Sophocles' death
- The commander's dream vision of Dionysus
- Sophocles' funeral rites and the metaphor of the poet as a Siren

## Main visual

A richly painted oblique reconstruction of the Theatre of Dionysus and its statue-lined approach in the Roman-period city Pausanias knew. The seated theatre, orchestra, stage building, poet statues, visitors, and the Acropolis above establish the place at first glance. Three distinct, fully draped statues in the foreground represent Menander, Euripides, and Sophocles; exact identification is added only by local labels and leaders. The scene is inhabited and archaeologically plausible, not a modern ruin or schematic plan.

## Scenic insets

1. **The commander's dream:** a fully clothed Spartan commander asleep in a restrained military camp in Attica while a dignified, fully robed Dionysus appears in a numinous but non-spectacular dream, gesturing toward a laurel or theatre mask associated with the unnamed “new Siren.”
2. **Honours for Sophocles:** a solemn, non-graphic Athenian funeral procession honouring the elderly poet, with fully clothed mourners, a covered bier, laurel, a theatre mask, and a small lyre. The image should convey civic and poetic honour without literalizing a mythological Siren as a monster.

## Orientation strategy

The passage panel includes a subordinate textured Athens relief locating the theatre on the Acropolis' south slope.

## Evidence boundary

Pausanias supplies the theatre statues, the poet names, the Lacedaemonian invasion, the commander's dream, Dionysus' instruction, the customary funeral honours, and the identification of Sophocles with the “new Siren.” The statue arrangement, theatre activity, camp setting, gestures, funeral route, objects, clothing, architecture, and visual form of the dream are reconstructions.
