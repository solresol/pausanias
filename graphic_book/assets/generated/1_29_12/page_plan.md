# 1.29.12 — The missing name

## What the page shows

On the Academy road outside Athens, Pausanias reads a casualty monument
that includes the Plataean allies and leaves out the general Nicias. He
attributes the explanation to Philistus and contrasts Demosthenes's terms
for his men with Nicias's own surrender. The heading locates the monument.
The picture shifts to the event Pausanias invokes. Its caption places the
surrender in Sicily in 413 BCE and identifies the scene as imagined.

Thucydides puts Nicias's surrender to Gylippus at the Assinarus and reports
Nicias asking that the killing of his soldiers cease. That setting allows
one scene without assuming we know the recorded gesture, clothing, exact
spot on the bank, or the monument's appearance. Pausanias's judgment and
the distinction involving Demosthenes remain in the complete translated
account. [Thucydides 7.84–85, Perseus Digital Library](https://beta.perseus.tufts.edu/urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0003.tlg001.perseus-eng4:7.85.1/)

## Three compositions

1. **Selected.** Title and continuous English share a horizontal reading
   band above one broad riverside painting. The reader encounters the name
   omitted in Athens before looking across the river at the surrender. The
   narrow caption anchors the Sicilian setting. One picture and no inset.
   Counting inverted image-and-prose arrangements in the same family, the
   layout occurs once in the candidate and five preceding pages. 1.29.6 falls
   just outside that window. The 1.29.10 and 1.29.11 layouts use an inset
   over a full-page scene and a central portrait with prose wings, respectively.
2. A three-panel sequence of the monument, Demosthenes's negotiation and
   Nicias's surrender, with matching text fields. It follows the order of
   the passage but turns an editorial judgment into a series of conjectural
   posed incidents and shrinks the river landscape.
3. One tall studio-like study of the lettering face of the monument with
   the text composing a band to its right. It could foreground absence but
   would require a speculative inscription and follow a page already centered
   on a close study of a stele.

## Geometry and art direction

A 3200 × 2630 page gives the complete English a single upper-right rectangle
of 1955 × 738 px. The title, passage ID and Athens orientation occupy the
left of the same band. The art is a 3020 × 1570 opening, about 1.92:1.
The caption has its own slim paper margin. Portable serif fonts, Pillow
wrapping, at least 21 px of padding and actual glyph bounds apply to each
field. The exact sentence order and punctuation must round-trip after line
wrapping. Minimum body size is 54 px. All lettering sits outside the raster.

The view will be at eye level and across the river, with Nicias and Gylippus
readable at medium distance, wet pebbles and reeds in the foreground, and
deep recession up the narrow valley. Compared with 1.29.11, a human encounter
at water level replaces the high close study of a stone. Cool silver-green
water, pale violet distance and graphite shadows replace sunlit terracotta
and ivory. Diffused daylight puts the emphasis on the people, wet cloth and
rock instead of hard-edged shadows. The river and distinct stances convey
the event without leaders or a locator. The caption identifies the moment
as an artistic reconstruction.

## Final comparison

The final PNG was reviewed as a complete page and in native-pixel crops,
then viewed beside the six accepted predecessors at thumbnail scale. The
one art rectangle and top reading band give it a distinct outline beside
the two immediate predecessors. 1.29.8 places prose both above and below
its illustration. Here the small bottom caption leaves the entire English
passage above the illustration. The family occurs once in 1.29.7–1.29.12.
The top half has no tall translation sidebar.

Both commanders, their onlookers and the separating stream stay legible
at thumbnail size. Native crops show the contrast between unarmed Nicias
and the resting equipment of Gylippus's group, with different textures
for linen, wool, bronze, rock and moving water. The eye-level, populated
streambank, cool palette and soft light all differ from the close, downward
study of sunlit stone in 1.29.11. The full text and reader checks are in
[review.md](review.md).
