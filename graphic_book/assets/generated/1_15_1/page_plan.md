# Page plan — Pausanias 1.15.1

## Passage

**1.15.1** — Pausanias approaches the Painted Stoa in the Athenian Agora. He
notes the bronze Hermes Agoraios, a nearby gate carrying an Athenian cavalry
trophy over Pleistarchus, and the stoa's painting of the opening movement of
the battle at Oinoe in Argive territory.

## Key places, events, and objects

- the Athenian Agora and the approach to the Stoa Poikile;
- the bronze Hermes Agoraios, a market landmark;
- the gate and trophy commemorating the defeat of Pleistarchus's cavalry;
- the Painted Stoa's battle picture;
- Oinoe in Argive territory, with Athenians and Lacedaemonians only beginning
  to close for combat.

## Main visual

A richly painted archaeological reconstruction of the north side of the
classical Athenian Agora. The view approaches the Stoa Poikile through an active
market, with the long painted colonnade as the architectural focus, Hermes
Agoraios in bronze in the foreground, and the trophy-bearing gate nearby.
Exact names and leaders are added locally.

## Insets

1. A passage-specific close study of the Oinoe wall painting: two ordered
   hoplite lines advancing at the beginning of battle, before the violent
   climax. This makes Pausanias's unusual temporal observation visible.
2. A subordinate oblique relief locator connecting Athens, the Saronic Gulf,
   the Argolid, and Oinoe. The route and exact labels are drawn locally.

## Orientation strategy

The main panel establishes the monuments relative to the Agora and Acropolis.
Callouts identify the Stoa Poikile, Hermes, and trophy gate. The locator inset
then moves from Athens southwest to Argive Oinoe, while its caption explicitly
distinguishes Oinoe in Argive territory from the better-known Attic deme.
