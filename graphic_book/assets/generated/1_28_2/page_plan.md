## Subjects, historical care and explicit art direction

Main: monumental bronze Athena on a conjectural Acropolis sanctuary terrace,
complete long drapery, helmet crest, spear tip, shield and plinth. Quiet standing
pose; all surfaces bronze. Broader environmental three-quarter view from low
human height: the whole spear and crest remain inside generous margins, with
open cobalt sky, pale stone and a restrained far Saronic sea horizon. No Nike,
owl, snake, invented event, modern ruins or specific reconstructed precinct
layout. Shield viewed obliquely, so do not fabricate a detailed Lapith/Centaur
relief or introduce nude combatants. This is a bronze dedication, not a living
goddess or the indoor gold-and-ivory Athena Parthenos.

Secondary: bronze four-horse chariot dedication, entirely static sculpted metal
on a plain stone plinth. No driver or living team. Medium-wide, mildly elevated
three-quarter view, full horses and vehicle inside crop, soft pale masonry
background. No battle, captives, inscription or exact original grouping claimed.
The four-horse interpretation has independent literary/epigraphical support,
but design, scale, setting and relative location remain conjectural. Do not
mistake another surviving chariot relief for this dedication.

Both components use sophisticated painterly realism with tactile bronze,
sculpted drapery or harness, worn stone, credible weight, layered atmospheric
depth. Main: mineral cobalt/ultramarine, dark umber bronze, pale ivory limestone,
clear dry late-morning light. Secondary: russet bronze, muted olive and chalk,
soft side light. No automatic elevated ochre sunset, flat vector terrain,
icon figures, schematic scenery, gratuitous nudity or pseudo-writing.
All headings, long text and caption live **outside** both rasters; no reserved
text holes inside either. Exact chosen aspects and viewpoint/light/palette go
into each prompt. Only selected composition commissioned.

Header names Athens/Acropolis. Sea horizon supports Pausanias's maritime
reference without pretending to reconstruct a sightline from Cape Sounion.
Footer explicitly marks lost forms, scale, setting and relative placement
uncertain. Exact prose retains Pericles and Lemnian Athena without pretending
the main helmeted spear-bearing statue depicts the separate Lemnian work.
Compared with 1.28.1: close single portrait to broader environment/two monuments;
damp clouded pearl/petrol to dry clear cobalt/umber/ivory; grey reflected light
to strong clear-day modelling. Distance, palette, light and object/environment
balance change for the passage, not arbitrary variety. Judge actual output.
