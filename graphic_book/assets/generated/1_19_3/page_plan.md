# Page plan — passage 1.19.3

## Passage

- ID: 1.19.3
- Exact English text: loaded at render time from `pausanias.sqlite`, table `translations`.

## Key places, events, and objects

- Cynosarges, a sanctuary of Heracles outside the Athenian city, associated by Pausanias with an oracle concerning a white dog.
- Altars to Heracles, Hebe, Alcmena, and Iolaus, expressing Heracles' divine household and heroic collaborator.
- The Lyceum, still regarded in Pausanias' day as a sanctuary of Apollo Lyceius and linked by name to Lycus son of Pandion.
- Lycus' flight from Aegeus to the Termilae and the reported renaming of that people as Lycians.

## Visual structure

- Main visual: a richly textured oblique reconstruction of the eastern and southeastern approaches to Roman-period Athens. The Acropolis anchors the distant city; the Ilissos crosses the landscape; a wooded Cynosarges precinct with a Heracles temple and altar court occupies the nearer southern bank; the Lyceum appears as a separate grove and sanctuary of Apollo to the east. Roads and terrain establish spatial relationships without claiming surveyed boundaries.
- Inset 1: a dramatic but restrained sanctuary moment showing a white dog beside a sacrificial precinct while priests consult an oracle tablet. The image illustrates Pausanias' allusion without inventing the oracle's lost wording or a specific foundation story.
- Inset 2: a richly reconstructed altar court at Cynosarges. Four distinct offering places evoke Heracles, Hebe, Alcmena, and Iolaus through appropriate cult objects and fully clothed worshippers; exact names are supplied only by local labels.
- Inset 3: Lycus, fully clothed and accompanied by a small travelling party, arriving through the rugged coastal mountains of southwest Anatolia among the Termilae. This illustrates the reported migration and renaming tradition without presenting it as historical fact.

## Orientation strategy

- The main panel uses the Acropolis and Ilissos as fixed landmarks and keeps Cynosarges and the Lyceum visually separate.
- Locally rendered leaders identify the Acropolis, Ilissos, Cynosarges, Heracles sanctuary, Lyceum grove, and Apollo Lyceius sanctuary.
- A local orientation strip states that the precinct placements are reconstructed; the Lycus inset extends the page's geography from Athens to Lycia.

## Evidence boundary

Pausanias records the sanctuary names, altar dedications, divine and heroic relationships, the Lyceum's continuing cult identity, and the Lycus-Termilae renaming story. The oracle's wording, sanctuary architecture, exact precinct boundaries, altar forms, ritual moment, and appearance of Lycus' journey are reconstructed. No generated inscription or invented oracle text is presented as evidence.
