# Page plan: Pausanias 1.17.4

- **Passage ID:** 1.17.4
- **Key places, events, and objects:** Theseus and Pirithous invading Thesprotian territory; the failed expedition and loss of most of their army; the Thesprotian king's capture of both companions; imprisonment at Cichyrus, also known in later sources as Ephyra; the north-west Greek landscape near the Acheron and Cocytus rivers.
- **Main visual:** A rich historical reconstruction of the captured Theseus and Pirithous, fully clothed and bound at the wrists, being brought through the stone gate court of a hill settlement at Cichyrus before the Thesprotian king. Wounded survivors, surrendered spears, mountain weather, and the defended gate make defeat and captivity immediately legible without relying on captions.
- **Insets:** (1) the Thesprotian counterattack breaking the invading column in a rugged river valley, explaining Pausanias' statement that most of the army was lost; (2) a quiet painterly approach to the fortified hill settlement above the Acheron plain, establishing what “held captive at Cichyrus” means spatially; (3) a subordinate textured relief view of north-west Greece, with the route from Athens toward Thesprotia and the Acheron–Cocytus meeting marked locally.
- **Orientation strategy:** The main scene establishes the fortified destination and the prisoners' status. The settlement approach inset links stronghold, river plain, mountains, and Ionian coast. The small relief inset gives regional orientation; exact route, place names, and callouts are rendered locally rather than entrusted to generated art.

- **Evidence boundary:** The royal court, fortification, battle arrangement, and garments are a historically informed reconstruction of Pausanias' account, not a claim about the excavated layout. Cichyrus/Ephyra's association with the Acheron and Cocytus landscape supplies geographical orientation; the disputed identification of a later monumental complex as a Nekromanteion is not asserted.
- **Content suitability:** All people are fully clothed. Captivity and battlefield defeat are shown without gore, exposed wounds, humiliation, or eroticized poses.
