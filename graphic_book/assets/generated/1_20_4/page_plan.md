# Page plan — Pausanias 1.20.4

- **Passage ID:** 1.20.4.
- **Key places, events, and objects:** the roofed odeion beside the sanctuary and Theatre of Dionysus; its many-columned, tent-like form associated with Xerxes; its destruction during Sulla's capture of Athens in 86 BCE; Mithridates VI's Pontic kingdom around the Euxine and expansion into Asia Minor.
- **Main visual:** a richly painted oblique reconstruction of the Odeion of Pericles immediately east of the Theatre of Dionysus, seen from the south-east. The broad, steep, pavilion-like roof is partly cut away to reveal the dense interior columns and seating, while the theatre, sanctuary precinct, and Acropolis establish the location. Exact names and interpretive cautions will be added locally.
- **Inset 1:** a historically grounded night tableau of Sulla's Roman assault on Athens in 86 BCE, with the odeion roof burning below the Acropolis; violence remains distant and non-graphic.
- **Inset 2:** an illuminated-relief orientation view from the Euxine/Black Sea through Pontus and Asia Minor toward the Aegean and Athens, with Mithridates and a small court/army group in the foreground. Exact place labels and route strokes will be rendered locally.
- **Orientation strategy:** the main panel fixes the building on Athens' south slope by the theatre and Acropolis. The second inset widens the scale from the Euxine to Athens and explains why Pausanias pauses at the building to begin the Mithridatic-war narrative.
- **Approved style anchors:** `graphic_book/images/1/1/4.png` and `graphic_book/images/1/1/5.png` for the parchment-atlas palette, sophisticated painterly finish, layered depth, and coffee-table-book ambition.
- **Continuity reference:** `graphic_book/images/1/20/3.png` for south-slope layout and adjacent sanctuary/theatre geography only; it does not supersede the approved anchors.
- **Evidence boundary:** Pausanias supplies the structure beside the sanctuary and theatre, its association with Xerxes' tent, rebuilding, Sulla's burning/capture, and Mithridates' Euxine realm and Asian conquests. The identification as the Odeion of Pericles is the conventional archaeological reading. Roof form, cutaway, interior arrangement, attire, exact flames, figures, and viewpoint are reconstructions. Ancient testimony differs on who set the fire during the siege, so the inset should show the destruction amid Sulla's capture without depicting Sulla personally igniting it.
- **Suitability:** all figures fully clothed; battle damage is distant; no bodies, gore, nudity, eroticized figures, logos, watermarks, or generated lettering.
