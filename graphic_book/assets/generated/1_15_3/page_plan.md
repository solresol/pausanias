# Page plan — Pausanias 1.15.3

## Passage

**1.15.3** — Pausanias reads the Marathon painting at the end of the Stoa
Poikile cycle. The composition moves from evenly matched Plataean, Athenian,
and Persian ranks to the Persian rout through the marsh and toward the
Phoenician ships. Divine and heroic figures share the field with the historical
commanders Callimachus and Miltiades, and with Echetlos.

## Key places, events, and objects

- the plain of Marathon between the mountains, marsh, and bay;
- Plataeans and the forces of Attica meeting the Persian army;
- the Persian flight into the marsh and toward the Phoenician fleet;
- Greek fighting at the ships;
- Marathon, Thereus rising from the earth, Athena, and Heracles;
- Callimachus, Miltiades, and Echetlos.

## Main visual

A rich oblique panorama of the Marathon plain from inland high ground toward
the bay. The battle reads across the landscape: ordered Greek and Persian ranks
meet in the centre, the Persian line breaks toward the marsh, and Phoenician
ships wait along the shore. Mountain ridges, cultivated plain, wetland, beach,
and sea make the geography immediately legible. Exact names, directional
leaders, and a battle-sequence route are added locally.

## Insets

1. A restrained heroic epiphany tableau: Marathon watches over the plain,
   Athena and Heracles stand as divine patrons, and Thereus emerges from the
   earth in an uncanny but non-gory moment. The generated-art prompt used the
   conventional identification “Theseus”; the exact local label follows the
   database translation’s “Thereus.”
2. A monumental-history close view of Callimachus, Miltiades, and Echetlos
   among the Greek line, with Plataean and Athenian contingents distinguishable
   through equipment and formation rather than unreliable generated text.

## Orientation strategy

The main panorama is both the visual centre and the locator: locally rendered
labels identify the inland heights, central engagement, marsh, bay, and
Phoenician ships. A locally drawn sequence line connects “evenly matched,”
“rout through the marsh,” and “fight at the ships.” A small compass and the
caption “Marathon, northeast Attica” provide regional orientation without
turning the page into a classroom map.
