# Page plan: Pausanias 1.18.3

- **Passage ID:** 1.18.3
- **Key places, people, and objects:** the Athenian Prytaneion; Solon's written laws; the goddesses Peace and Hestia; the civic hearth; Autolykos the pancratiast; statues originally honouring Miltiades and Themistocles whose inscriptions were later changed to identify a Roman and a Thracian.
- **Main visual:** A richly painted archaeological reconstruction of the Prytaneion interior as a dignified civic and sacred hall. The central flame of Hestia, displayed law tablets, and standing figures of Peace and Hestia form a clear visual hierarchy, while rows of honorific statues recede into the architecture. The scene conveys the institution's combined legal, sacred, and commemorative functions without claiming a securely known plan.
- **Insets:** (1) a museum-quality object and workshop study of two old honorific statues as masons cut replacement inscriptions into their bases, expressing the afterlife of the Miltiades and Themistocles monuments without relying on generated lettering; (2) a sculptural study of Autolykos as a disciplined, fully clothed pancratiast in the Prytaneion's honorific setting; (3) a subordinate, richly textured oblique relief of ancient central Athens showing the Acropolis, Agora, and a deliberately broad conjectural Prytaneion zone.
- **Orientation strategy:** The main scene labels the civic hearth, law display, Peace, Hestia, and honorific-statue gallery. The relief establishes the Prytaneion within central Athens but marks its location only as a reconstructed zone. A bottom strap summarizes the transition from civic memory to Roman-era re-identification.
