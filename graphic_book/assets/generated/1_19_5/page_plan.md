# Page plan — Pausanias 1.19.5

## Passage

- **Passage ID:** 1.19.5
- **Key places:** Athens, the Ilissus, the Athenian Eridanus, the Ilissus–Eridanus confluence, and the Magnesian coast where storms damaged the Persian fleet.
- **Key events and objects:** Oreithyia playing beside the Ilissus; Boreas carrying her away and taking her as his wife; the Athenians' belief that Boreas later aided them against the Persian fleet; the altar of the Ilissiad Muses; and the traditional place where the Peloponnesians killed King Codrus.

## Visual design

- **Main visual:** a richly painted oblique reconstruction of southeast Athens, following the Ilissus valley toward the city and Acropolis. The Ilissus is the main spatial axis; the smaller Eridanus joins it visibly. A modest riverside altar marks the Ilissiad Muses, and a separate memorial marker indicates the traditional Codrus site. Exact names and leaders are added locally.
- **Inset 1:** Boreas, personified as a powerful winged North Wind, lifting the fully clothed Oreithyia from the Ilissus riverbank. The river landscape and her interrupted play make the episode legible without relying on the caption.
- **Inset 2:** Persian triremes breaking formation in a violent north wind off the rocky Magnesian coast, with damaged ships and heavy seas but no bodies or graphic suffering.
- **Orientation strategy:** local labels identify Athens, Acropolis, Ilissus, Eridanus, their confluence, the Muses' altar, and Codrus's traditional death site. A small locally rendered orientation strip links Athens and the Boreas tradition to the distant fleet disaster off Magnesia.

## Evidence and suitability

- Pausanias supplies the named rivers, the Oreithyia and Boreas story, the claimed aid against the fleet, the altar, and the Codrus tradition. River courses, confluence viewpoint, altar form, memorial form, clothing, exact fleet location, weather, and architecture are reconstructed.
- All figures are fully clothed. The abduction is shown as mythic wind-borne motion without eroticization, exposed bodies, or injury. The fleet scene contains no gore or visible casualties.
- Generated art contains no exact text; all passage text, captions, labels, and leaders are drawn locally with measured layout.
