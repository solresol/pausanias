# Page plan — Pausanias 1.15.2

## Passage

**1.15.2** — Pausanias reads two adjacent paintings in the Stoa Poikile. The
first shows Athenians and Theseus fighting the Amazons, whose wider story runs
from the fall of Themiscyra through the failed attack on Athens to their later
appearance at Troy. The next shows the Greeks after the capture of Ilium: the
kings assemble over Ajax's outrage against Cassandra, with Ajax, Cassandra,
and captive Trojan women included in the painting.

## Key places, events, and objects

- the Painted Stoa as the architectural viewing context;
- Theseus and the Athenians fighting the Amazons;
- Themiscyra, Athens, and Troy as the Amazons' geographic sequence;
- the captured city of Ilium;
- the assembled Greek kings, Ajax, Cassandra, and the captive women;
- Cassandra's sanctuary refuge and the moral reckoning after the sack.

## Main visual

A richly painted archaeological reconstruction of the shaded interior of the
Stoa Poikile. Two monumental wall paintings dominate the gallery: an
Amazonomachy at left and the Greek council after the sack of Troy at right.
Visitors remain subordinate, giving scale and showing how Pausanias encountered
the works. Exact names and explanatory leaders are added locally.

## Insets

1. A close Amazonomachy tableau with Theseus and fully armoured or fully clothed
   women fighting in disciplined formations outside Athens.
2. A restrained post-sack tableau: Cassandra, fully clothed, beside Athena's
   sanctuary image while Ajax stands under accusation and the Greek kings
   assemble. There is no assault, nudity, gore, or sensational violence.
3. A subordinate relief-atlas strip linking Themiscyra, Athens, and Troy to
   orient Pausanias's compressed chronology.

## Orientation strategy

The main panel establishes the paintings inside the Athenian stoa and labels
their reading order. The atlas strip then separates the three places named or
implied in the Amazon history. A short sequence panel distinguishes the two
paintings and makes the transition from battle to moral reckoning explicit.
