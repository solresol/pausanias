# Page plan: passage 1.16.3

- **Passage ID:** 1.16.3
- **Key places, events, and objects:** Seleucus's justice and piety; the bronze Apollo taken by Xerxes from Branchidae to Ecbatana and restored to the Milesians; the foundation of Seleucia beside the Tigris; the transfer of Babylonians to the new city; Babylon's wall, sanctuary of Bel, and Chaldean community left intact.
- **Main visual:** a richly painted oblique reconstruction of newly founded Seleucia on the Tigris, seen from the river approach as Babylonian families, merchants, boats, surveyors, and builders enter a planned Hellenistic city. The broad Tigris, gridded streets, walls, quays, and Mesopotamian plain provide immediate historical and spatial orientation.
- **Insets:** (1) a sophisticated relief atlas connecting Branchidae in Ionia with Ecbatana in Media and the paired Mesopotamian cities of Babylon and Seleucia; (2) a ceremonial, historically grounded return of a monumental bronze Apollo to the sanctuary at Branchidae; (3) Babylon's wall and sanctuary of Bel still active, with Chaldean priests and scholars continuing their work.
- **Orientation strategy:** exact local labels identify the Tigris, Seleucia, and the arriving Babylonian community in the main panel; the atlas shows the long east-west restitution route and the shorter Babylon-to-Seleucia transfer; the lower scenic panels contrast restoration at Branchidae with preservation at Babylon.
