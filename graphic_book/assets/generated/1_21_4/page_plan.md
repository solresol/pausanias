# Page plan — Pausanias 1.21.4

## Passage

`1.21.4`

Pausanias continues upward from the Theatre of Dionysus along the Acropolis south slope. He identifies the grave of Kalos on the road, recalls Daedalus' killing of his nephew and pupil and subsequent flight through Crete to Cocalus in Sicily, then enters the sanctuary of Asclepius with its divine family statues, paintings, and spring. At the spring he locates the killing of Halirrhothius by Ares after the assault on Alcippe, and the tradition of Athens' first homicide trial.

## Key places, events, people, and objects

- The ascent from the Theatre of Dionysus toward the Acropolis
- Kalos' roadside grave and the memory of Daedalus' crime
- Daedalus' flight from Athens to Crete and then to Cocalus in Sicily
- The south-slope Asclepieion, its colonnades, statues, paintings, and spring
- Asclepius and his children represented as cult statues
- Ares' killing of Halirrhothius and the first homicide trial, shown through the trial rather than the assault or killing

## Main visual

A rich, oblique historical reconstruction of the inhabited Acropolis south-slope ascent, continuing the geography established in 1.21.3. The Theatre of Dionysus anchors the lower foreground; a stone road climbs past a modest roadside grave marker for Kalos into the terraced Asclepieion. The sanctuary occupies the visual heart of the page, with colonnades, votive paintings, fully draped statues of Asclepius and his children, and a spring emerging from the rock. The Acropolis wall rises above, giving the reader immediate vertical and geographic orientation.

## Scenic insets

1. **Daedalus and Kalos:** a sophisticated workshop scene before the crime, showing master and gifted young pupil studying compasses, saw, and carved wooden mechanisms. A shadowed doorway and packed travel bundle foreshadow Daedalus' flight without depicting the killing. All figures are fully clothed and no violence is shown.
2. **The first homicide trial:** a solemn, historically styled council scene on the rocky Areopagus. Fully clothed Ares stands before seated Athenian judges while Alcippe is present under family protection. The image shows legal reckoning, not the assault or killing; no weapons are raised, and there is no injury, nudity, or eroticization.

## Orientation strategy

The main reconstruction presents a legible lower-to-upper sequence: `THEATRE · KALOS' GRAVE · ASCLEPIEION · ACROPOLIS`. Local callouts identify the road, grave, sanctuary colonnade, divine-family statues, paintings, and spring. A compact relief-based route strip in the passage panel connects Athens, Crete, and Sicily as the reported stages of Daedalus' flight; it remains subordinate to the main architectural landscape.

## Evidence boundary

Pausanias supplies the road's endpoints, Kalos' burial and relationship to Daedalus, the flight via Crete to Cocalus in Sicily, the Asclepieion's statues, paintings and spring, and the spring's association with the killing and first homicide trial. Exact architecture, grave form, statue arrangement, artworks, route geometry, clothing, activity, lighting, and trial composition are reconstructions. The assault and killing are deliberately not depicted.
