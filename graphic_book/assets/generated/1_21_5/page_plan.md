# Page plan: passage 1.21.5

## Passage

**ID:** 1.21.5

Pausanias describes a displayed Sarmatian breastplate and explains how an
iron-poor, geographically isolated horse people used bone spear and dart points,
horn bows, and thrown ropes that entangled enemies before the riders wheeled
their horses and pulled them down.

## Key places, events, and objects

- The Sarmatian breastplate seen by Pausanias among displayed objects in Athens.
- A scale-like cuirass assembled from many small, carefully fitted plates.
- Horn bows, bone-tipped arrows, spears, and darts as substitutes for iron.
- Mounted rope tactics: cast, catch, wheel the horse, and overturn the opponent.
- Geographic separation between Athens and the north-Pontic/steppe world.

## Main visual

A rich, close archaeological reconstruction of the Sarmatian breastplate on a
wooden display support in a softly lit Greek sanctuary-gallery setting. The
armour is visually dominant: overlapping pale horn/hoof-like scales, leather
bindings, reinforced shoulders, and precise handwork. A receding colonnade and
small offerings establish the Athenian viewing context without turning the panel
into a schematic museum diagram.

## Scenic insets

1. **Materials and craft:** a fully clothed Sarmatian craftsperson in a timber
   and felt workshop shaping bone points and assembling a horn bow, with the raw
   materials and finished weapons visibly related.
2. **Mounted rope tactic:** two fully clothed Sarmatian horsemen on open steppe;
   the nearer rider has cast a rope around an armed opponent and wheels his horse
   to pull the entangled man off balance. The action is dynamic but non-graphic.

## Orientation strategy

A subordinate, richly textured relief map runs from Athens and the Aegean to the
north shore of the Black Sea and the steppe. Local overlays will add exact labels
and a route/connection line; the map is explicitly secondary to the breastplate.
Callouts on the main panel identify the scale construction, leather lacing,
reinforced shoulder, and display context. Insets receive leaders to the relevant
tool, horn bow, rope loop, and wheeling horse.

## Evidence boundary and suitability

Pausanias supplies the displayed breastplate, Sarmatian isolation and lack of
iron, bone points, horn bows, arrows, darts, ropes, and the wheel-and-overturn
tactic. The breastplate's exact scale material and construction details, the
Athenian display architecture, workshop arrangement, clothing, terrain,
lighting, and individual participants are reconstructions. All figures must be
fully clothed; the combat scene must contain no gore or visible injury.
