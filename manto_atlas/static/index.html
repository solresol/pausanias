MEASUREMENT, NOT MYTH-MAKING

What this preview can tell us

The comparison

Geographic neighbours are selected by great-circle distance, independently of narrative resemblance. A figure is counted once per place across selected foundation, eponym, rule, cult, burial, games and patronage relations.

Rare figures receive more weight than ubiquitous ones. Similarity is weighted shared figures divided by the weighted union. Local difference is one minus distance-weighted average similarity among neighbours with observed profiles.

Empty profiles remain unobserved, not perfectly distinctive. No independent-source count or confidence interval is claimed yet.

The limits

  • Current source-date flags are provisional; the cutoff is 180 CE, not a genuinely earlier landmark.
  • Representative coordinates may stand for uncertain or non-settlement locations.
  • Source volume and literary selection can create apparent uniqueness.
  • City versus village requires independently dated historical evidence.
  • Automated passage claims may confuse monuments, earlier events and habitation.

Historical model results, not a new model

The corrected July study used 255 unique places, 204 positives and 51 negatives. It removed Pausanias-derived attention predictors and duplicate identities crossing folds. Its weak result has not yet been geographically validated.

These runs used the older March release. They are not predictions for the displayed release. Higher earlier scores were retired.

Graph neural network: planned benchmark

Compare a small relation-aware GNN with geography, evidence-volume and logistic baselines on the same spatially held-out settlements. Separate geographic and narrative edges; withhold endpoint text and labels; include feature-only and shuffled-graph controls. A GNN will be retained only if it adds reliable held-out value.

The next research gate is an 80–120-place, two-region audit of identities, independent witness dates and endpoint habitation. Until then, this is an evidence explorer, not a classifier or copying detector.
